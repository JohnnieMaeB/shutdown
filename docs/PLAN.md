# Shutdown: plan

The living version of this plan is a Claude doc ("Shutdown: Plan"). This copy keeps the parts the code refers to.

## Who it's for

Autistic adults who speak most days but sometimes can't: during shutdown, sensory overload, burnout, or after a long day of talking. It's not for full-time AAC users, who need complete systems like Proloquo or TD Snap, and it makes no clinical claims.

## First version

Show or say a short message within seconds of taking out the phone, while overloaded, with no setup at that moment. Offline, no account, no AI, everything on the phone.

1. **One-tap start:** Android Quick Settings tile and home-screen shortcut; iOS widget and Action button shortcut. Not built yet.
2. **Phrase board:** 12 starter phrases as large emoji-and-word tiles, grouped by place (Main, Out, Health, Work, Home); editable. Built.
3. **Show mode by default:** the phrase fills the screen in big high-contrast text; Speak is one more tap; Flip for someone across a table. Built.
4. **Type to speak:** one text box with suggestions from your own past phrases. Not built yet.
5. **Talk signal:** green, yellow and red cards, and a ready-made text to a trusted person through the share sheet. Not built yet.
6. **Low-demand design:** no streaks, reminders or badges; a light buzz instead of sounds; large touch targets. Built into what exists.

## Testing with autistic adults

6 to 8 paid autistic adults who sometimes lose speech, recruited through autistic-led groups and following AASPIRE's guidelines. Text-only participation is always possible, and nobody is watched during a real shutdown. Steps: learn the moments, try a prototype (testers rewrite the starter phrases), two to four weeks of real use with a three-tap diary, then a debrief. Working means a locked phone to the first phrase in under 5 seconds, and at least 4 of 6 people using it in a real moment and wanting to keep it.
